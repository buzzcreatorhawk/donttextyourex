# How Do I Stop Checking My Ex's Instagram?

**Make it require effort.** Willpower is not the tool for this, because willpower
is lowest at exactly the hour you reach for the app. Friction works: mute her,
then unfollow, then take the app off your phone — gradually in that order if you
need to, all three tonight if you can stand it.

That's the whole mechanism. The rest of this is why checking costs you more than
texting does, what you're actually looking for when you open her profile, and
what to do at the moment your thumb starts moving on its own.

---

## Why can't I stop checking her Instagram?

Because checking pays out at random, and a random payout is the most
habit-forming shape there is. Most times you look there's nothing — a coffee, a
friend's birthday, a sunset. Occasionally there's something that lands like a
punch, and your brain remembers that one and files the forty empty checks as the
price of admission.

That is the same structure as a fruit machine, and it is why the habit doesn't
respond to being told it's stupid. You already know it's stupid. Knowing has
never once stopped you, because the knowing happens in a different part of your
head from the reaching.

There's a second reason, and it's the one men admit less often. Checking feels
like doing something. It has the shape of action — gathering information, staying
informed, keeping a hand in — and so it holds off the feeling of having been made
passive, which is one of the worst parts of being left. It is the cheapest
available impression of still being involved.

## Is checking her Instagram worse than texting her?

**In one specific way, yes: a text ends and checking doesn't.** You send a
message and it either gets a reply or it doesn't, and either way that particular
event is over. Opening her profile has no end state at all. You can do it forty
times in a day and never once arrive at the thing you're looking for.

It's also rigged against you in a way a conversation isn't. What you see is a
selected frame — the fifteen good seconds out of her week, chosen and filtered by
someone who wants to be seen doing well. What you're comparing it against is the
inside of your own worst hour, unedited, live. That comparison has exactly one
possible verdict, and you already know which way it goes.

So the ninety seconds of relief that texting buys you, checking doesn't even buy.
It's the cost with none of the payment.

## What am I actually looking for?

One of two things, and neither of them is on the page. Either you want evidence
that she's struggling too — that the thing you had was real and it cost her
something — or you want evidence that she's completely gone, so you can stop
hoping and get on with it.

Her profile cannot give you the first, because nobody posts that. And it cannot
give you the second either, because no photograph is conclusive enough to switch
off hope. You'll find a way to read a new haircut as grief and a night out as a
message aimed at you. Both readings are available in every single post, which is
exactly why you keep going back.

Here's the test for whether this is a habit or a search: a search ends when you
find the thing. This one never ends. So it isn't a search.

## Should I mute her, unfollow her, or block her?

All three do different jobs, so choose by how bad it actually is rather than by
what feels proportionate.

**Mute her** if she's reaching you through the feed — stories at the top of the
screen, posts arriving unbidden. Muting costs nothing socially, she is not told,
and it removes the accidental encounters. It does nothing at all about the
deliberate ones.

**Unfollow her** if you find yourself on her profile without having decided to go
there. Unfollowing takes her out of the places you arrive at by drift. It is also
visible, and you have to decide you don't mind that, which is its own bit of
useful work.

**Block her** if you are typing her name into the search bar. That's the line.
Mute and unfollow both leave the profile two taps away; blocking is the only one
of the three that removes the route. If you have ever logged out of your own
account to look at hers, or looked from a second account, or opened a friend's
story to see whether she was in it — you are past muting. Block.

Don't announce any of it. A public unfollow with a caption is a message, and
sending messages is the thing you're trying to stop.

And if you notice real resistance to muting her — some part of you arguing that
you need the access — that resistance is the answer. You aren't keeping the
profile for information. You're keeping the option.

## What if I need to know whether she's seeing someone?

You don't need to know. You want to know, and finding out does not help you,
which is a different sentence from the one you were hoping for.

Play both outcomes forward honestly. If she isn't seeing anyone, you get a few
days of relief and a quiet return of hope — and you'll be back on the profile
within the week to re-confirm it. If she is, you get a piece of information you
cannot unlearn, at 2am, alone, with no way to act on it, and it will run on a
loop for a fortnight.

One outcome extends this and the other detonates it. That is the actual choice on
offer. There is no third door where the information sets you free, even though
the entire reason you keep looking is that you believe there is one.

## "What if blocking her makes me look bitter?"

Nobody is watching this as closely as you think. The audience you're worried
about — mutual friends, her sister, the group chat — has its own life running at
full volume and is not auditing your follow list.

And the reading you're afraid of has it backwards. Blocking is not a statement
about her; it's a door you close because you keep walking through it. Men who
have been through this recognise that instantly. The people who read it as
bitterness are, almost without exception, people who have never needed to do it.

If it genuinely matters to you, mute plus unfollow gets you most of the distance
with none of the visibility. Just be honest about whether you chose that because
it's enough, or because it leaves the door open.

## Does checking her stories still count as breaking no contact?

**Yes — story-watching is contact, and it's the loophole that wastes the whole
month.** Thirty days of not texting while watching everything she posts is not
distance. It's contact with extra steps: you take on all of the information and
none of the resolution, and at the end of it you're thirty days older in the same
spot.

## What do I do at the moment I reach for the app?

Assume you will not win this by deciding harder. Build the obstacles now, while
you're calm, so the version of you at 2am runs into them instead of into a
choice.

- **Delete the app, keep the account.** You lose nothing permanent and you gain a
  login screen between the urge and the profile. If you need Instagram for work,
  use it in a browser — it's deliberately worse, and worse is the point.
- **Log out every time.** If deleting it is a step too far, log out when you're
  done. The habit runs on a gesture that takes under a second, and a password
  breaks the gesture.
- **Move the icon.** Off the home screen, off page one, into a folder with a dull
  name. A surprising amount of this is muscle memory pointed at a position on
  glass.
- **Put a screen-time limit on it and let someone else set the code.** Your own
  passcode is not a limit; it's a speed bump you know the height of.
- **Sleep with the phone in another room.** Buy a cheap alarm clock — the reason
  the phone is by the bed is the alarm, and the alarm is the easy part to
  replace. Most of the damage happens between midnight and three, in bed, in the
  dark.
- **Give the thumb somewhere else to go.** The reach is real and it wants a
  destination. A paper book on the bedside table works better than it has any
  right to.

None of this is self-improvement and none of it has to feel noble tonight. It's
interruption. Break the gesture, then do it again tomorrow.

## What if I've already looked tonight?

Then you looked. That's one check, and one check does not undo three weeks —
treat it the way you'd treat one missed session at the gym, not as evidence that
the whole attempt was a fantasy.

What actually does the damage is the second move: the scroll back through two
years of photographs, the zoom on a stranger's face at the edge of a frame, the
hour of forensics. The single check costs you about ten minutes. The forensics
cost you the night and set you back days.

So the rule isn't don't look. You're going to look. The rule is: the moment you
notice you're looking, phone face down, stand up. That's the whole intervention,
and it's available to you mid-scroll.

## How long until the urge goes away?

Weeks, and it comes down in steps rather than on a slope. Most men find the
compulsive phase — several checks a day, the first thing before getting out of
bed — starts loosening somewhere in the third or fourth week of real distance,
and real distance means distance from the profile and not only from her number.

It does not fade evenly. You'll get four clean days and then a Thursday where you
look eleven times for no reason you can name. That isn't a relapse and it isn't
proof the habit is permanent; it's what the process looks like from the inside.
The same pattern runs through the whole of a breakup, and it's covered properly
here: [Why does a breakup hurt more than you expected?](/why-does-a-breakup-hurt-so-much/)

The urge going quiet is not quite the goal, either. The goal is that her profile
stops being the place you go when you feel bad. Once something else is there
instead, it becomes what it actually is, which is a stranger's photo album.

## If it isn't moving

Some of this is just grief, and it shifts with time and a few blunt habits. Some
of it doesn't.

If the checking has become hours rather than minutes, or it's reaching into your
work and your sleep for weeks on end, that's a signal rather than a character
flaw. If your car wouldn't start you'd take it to a mechanic without a second
thought about what it said about you; your head is no different. If cost is the
barrier — and it's a real barrier — free and low-cost options exist, and a search
will find them.

## The short version

- **Friction beats willpower.** Mute, then unfollow, then take the app off the
  phone.
- **Checking is worse than texting in one way:** a text ends, and checking has no
  end state at all.
- **You're comparing your worst hour to her best fifteen seconds.** That
  comparison has one verdict built into it.
- **Block if you're typing her name in.** That's the line where mute and unfollow
  have stopped being enough.
- **Knowing whether she's seeing someone doesn't help either way.** One answer
  extends this, the other detonates it.
- **Watching her stories is contact.** It's the loophole that wastes the month.
- **When you catch yourself looking: phone face down, stand up.** The forensics
  are the expensive part, not the check.
