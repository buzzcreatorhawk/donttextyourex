# How Do I Stop Thinking About My Ex?

**Stop trying to stop.** "Don't think about her" is an instruction your head
cannot follow, because checking whether you're succeeding means thinking about
her. What works is giving the thought somewhere specific to go instead, and
shrinking the number of places it can find you.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this is why suppression backfires, what the
replaying is actually doing, and what to do in the minute a thought of her
arrives.

---

## Why can't I stop thinking about her?

Because she was wired into everything. Not only the big things: the route home,
the song in the shop, the joke you'd have sent her, the half of the bed, the
name at the top of your messages. Every one of those is a cue, and for months
or years each cue led to her. The cues are still there. The road at the end of
them is the only thing that's gone.

So the thoughts are not a sign that you're weak or that you're still in love in
some way that means you should go back. They're a sign that your daily life was
built around a person, and it takes a while to rebuild it around something else.
That takes weeks of ordinary days, not a decision.

## Why does trying not to think about her make it worse?

**Because suppression has a rebound built into it.** In a 1987 experiment,
people were asked to talk for five minutes without thinking about a white bear,
and to ring a bell every time they did. They kept ringing the bell. And
afterwards, when they were allowed to think about anything, they thought about
the bear more than people who had been free to think about it from the start.

The mechanism is simple once you see it. To make sure you're not thinking about
her, part of your mind has to keep checking, and the checking holds her name
right where you can see it. Every time you catch yourself and push her away, you
have just thought about her twice.

The same study had one finding that matters more. In a second experiment, people
given a specific other thing to think about instead (a red Volkswagen) were
spared most of the rebound. Not "think about something else". One concrete
thing, chosen in advance.

The study was about bears, not exes, and it was a lab task, not a breakup. But
"just stop thinking about her" has exactly the same structure, and it fails for
the same reason. The white bear is one of the studies in this episode of The
2AM Guy, which follows one night of checking her profile minute by minute:
[Why You Can't Stop Checking Her Instagram at 2 AM?](/videos/instagram-spiral/)

## What should I think about instead?

Something specific, decided now, while you're calm. The version of you who is
mid-replay at 1am will not come up with a good alternative on the spot, so don't
leave it to him.

It works best when it's concrete and has a next step:

- **A problem with moving parts.** The shelf you've been meaning to put up, a
  route you'd like to run, a piece of work you can actually advance. Something
  your head can chew on.
- **A plan for the next day.** What you'll eat, when you'll train, who you'll
  message. Small, practical, about you.
- **Something with your hands.** Thoughts loop worst when your body is idle. A
  walk, the dishes, a press-up set on the bedroom floor.

The point is not to feel better instantly. The point is that when the thought
comes, it meets a well-worn alternative instead of an empty room.

## Is it normal to replay the relationship over and over?

Yes, and it helps to know what the replaying is trying to do. Your head is
looking for the moment it went wrong, on the assumption that if you can find it,
you can understand it, and if you can understand it, it will stop hurting.

That search almost never ends, because breakups rarely have one moment. They
have a slow drift, a few bad weeks, a conversation that was the end of something
that had already ended. You can rewind the tape a hundred times and keep finding
new candidates for the moment, which is exactly why the tape keeps rewinding.

Here's the test: replaying that produces a decision ("I need to say what I want
sooner next time") has done its job. Replaying that produces only the next
replay is a loop, and the loop is not going to hand you anything on the
hundred-and-first pass that it didn't on the hundredth.

## What do I do in the minute she comes into my head?

Assume the thought will arrive. You can't stop it arriving; you can only choose
the next ten seconds.

- **Name it.** "That's her again." Plainly, without arguing with it. Naming a
  thought puts a small distance between you and it.
- **Don't follow it.** You don't have to finish the scene, open the photo, or
  work out what she's doing tonight. Let it sit there unfinished.
- **Go to the thing you picked.** The specific one, from the list you made when
  you were calm.
- **Move.** Stand up, change rooms, open a window. A change of position breaks
  a loop faster than a change of mind.

You will do this badly at first, and many times a day. That's fine. It's a rep,
not a test.

## Should I give myself time to think about her?

Oddly, yes. A fixed slot works better than an open door.

Pick fifteen minutes, early evening, the same time each day, never in bed. When
she comes into your head at other times, tell yourself "not now, at six". At six,
sit down and actually think it through: write it, rant it into a notes app,
let it be as bad as it is. When the fifteen minutes are up, stop and do something
physical.

What this does is take away the urgency. The thoughts lose their power to
interrupt when they know they'll get their turn. Many men find that by the
second week the slot starts going unused, which is its own kind of evidence.

## Does seeing her online keep the thoughts going?

**Yes, and more than almost anything else.** Every story, every post, every
photo in someone else's feed is a fresh cue delivered to your hand. You cannot
let the thoughts settle while you're feeding them new material several times a
day.

Mute, unfollow or block, depending on how deep the habit runs. The full version,
including which of the three you actually need, is here:
[How do I stop checking my ex's Instagram?](/how-to-stop-checking-your-exs-instagram/)

The same goes for the old message thread. If you reread it, archive it somewhere
that takes effort to reach. You're not deleting the history. You're taking it
off the route you walk every day.

## Why is it always worst at night?

Because night strips out everything that holds the thoughts back. No work, no
people, no noise, a tired brain with its guard down, and a phone a few inches
from your face. The thoughts were there at 3pm as well; at 3am there's nothing
else in the room with them.

So make night the most structured part of your day, not the least. Phone out of
the bedroom, the specific thing ready on the bedside table, and a rule that
nothing she-related gets decided after midnight. A thought that feels urgent at
2am almost always looks different at 11am with coffee in front of you.

## How long until I stop thinking about her all the time?

Weeks to a few months for the constant version, and it fades in steps, not a
smooth slope. First she stops being the first thought of the morning. Then
there are stretches of an hour, then an afternoon, when you realise she hasn't
come up at all. Then a song in a shop catches you out on a good week and it
feels like you've gone back to the start.

You haven't. That's what the process looks like from the inside, and the reason
it doesn't move in a straight line is covered here:
[Why does a breakup hurt more than you expected?](/why-does-a-breakup-hurt-so-much/)

The goal isn't never thinking about her again. You had a real part of your life
with her, and it will come to mind for years, the way any big chapter does. The
goal is that the thought arrives, passes through, and doesn't take the evening
with it.

## If it isn't moving

Some of this is just grief, and it shifts with time and a few blunt habits. Some
of it doesn't.

If the thoughts are taking hours of your day, keeping you from sleeping for
weeks on end, or starting to sound like you'd be better off not being here at
all, that's a signal rather than a character flaw. If your car wouldn't start
you'd take it to a mechanic without a second thought about what it said about
you; your head is no different. If cost is the barrier, and it's a real barrier,
free and low-cost options exist, and a search will find them. If you're in
danger tonight, contact your local emergency number.

---

## The short version

- **"Don't think about her" can't work.** Checking that you're not thinking
  about her is thinking about her.
- **Suppression rebounds.** In the white bear study, the avoided thought came
  back stronger afterwards.
- **A specific alternative helps.** One concrete thing, picked in advance, not
  "something else".
- **Replaying is a search with no finish line.** If it isn't producing a
  decision, it's a loop.
- **Give the thoughts a slot.** Fifteen minutes, same time, never in bed.
- **Stop feeding the cues.** Her profile is new material delivered to your hand.
- **It fades in steps.** A bad day on a good week is not going back to the start.
