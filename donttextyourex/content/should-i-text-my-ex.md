# Should I Text My Ex?

**No. Not tonight, and not for the reason you're telling yourself.**

The urge to text is almost never about her. It's about wanting the discomfort to
stop. Sending the message trades a week of slow healing for about ninety seconds
of relief, and then restarts the clock.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this is why it's true, what the text will
actually get you, and what to do with your hands instead.

---

## What's actually happening at 2am?

You're not sitting there with a considered point to make. If you were, it could
wait until Tuesday.

What's happening is that the discomfort has reached a level where your brain has
started generating reasons to make it stop. That's all a craving is — a feeling
that arrives first and recruits an argument afterwards. The argument feels like
yours. It isn't. It's the feeling wearing a suit.

You can test this. Whatever reason you've got for texting her right now — write
it down, and then read it at 11am tomorrow with coffee in front of you. If it
still looks like a reason, it was one. It almost never still looks like a reason.

## What will the text actually get you?

Play it forward honestly. There are three ways it goes.

**She doesn't reply.** Now you're where you were, plus the knowledge that you
reached out and got nothing, plus the several hours you'll spend watching for
the reply. This is the most common outcome and it's strictly worse than silence
you chose.

**She replies politely.** Something warm and short. You'll read it forty times
looking for the thing that isn't in it. This feels like the good outcome for
about a day, and then it's worse than no reply, because now there's a thread and
you're going to want to keep it alive.

**She replies the way you're hoping.** Now you're back in it — but back in it
from the floor, with none of the standing you had before, on terms she sets.
Whatever ended the relationship is still there, and you've now demonstrated that
you'll break your own rules at 2am.

Look at those three again. Two are bad and one is a trap. That's the actual
distribution, and some part of you already knows it, which is why you're reading
this instead of typing.

## "But I just want closure"

Closure isn't a thing another person can hand you.

The version of closure you're imagining involves her saying something that makes
this make sense — an explanation good enough that the feeling switches off. That
conversation doesn't exist. If she had an explanation that clean, she'd have
given it to you already.

Here's the part that's harder to hear: she's probably already moved on. Not
because she didn't care. Because she started leaving before you knew there was
any leaving happening — emotionally, mentally, weeks or months before the words.
By the time she ended it, she'd already grieved it. You hadn't. That's the gap
you're standing in, and it's why she seems fine and you're on the floor.

You can't close that gap with a text. You close it by doing your grieving on
your own schedule, starting now, instead of waiting for hers to be handed back
to you.

## "What if she texted me first?"

Different situation, same answer, for a different reason.

A message from her doesn't obligate you to anything. You're allowed to read it
and not reply tonight. Waiting isn't a tactic and it isn't a game — it's the
gap between the feeling and the decision, which is exactly the gap you didn't
have when you were about to text her at 2am.

If it's logistics — her stuff, your stuff, something shared — answer it, keep it
to the logistics, and don't append the paragraph. You know the paragraph. It's
the one that starts as a sentence about a jumper and ends up being about the
relationship.

If it's not logistics, you can leave it. "Seen and not answered" is a complete
sentence.

## How long is this supposed to last?

Longer than you want, and less than it feels like tonight.

The honest answer is that nobody can give you a number, and anyone selling you
one — thirty days, ninety days — is selling you a number because you want one,
not because it's true. What actually matters isn't the count. It's whether the
distance is being used for anything.

Thirty days of not texting while checking her Instagram every night isn't no
contact. It's contact with extra steps, and it will leave you exactly where you
started, thirty days older.

## What should I do with the urge instead?

The urge is real and it needs somewhere to go. Willpower alone is a bad plan at
2am, because willpower is the thing that's lowest at 2am.

So don't fight it. Aim it.

**Do something physical, right now.** Fifty push-ups. Three times, if once
doesn't do it. It sounds stupid, it's supposed to sound stupid, and it works
because it's very hard to hold a spiral together while you're out of breath.

**Take a cold shower.** Not because it's trendy. Because for those ninety
seconds your entire nervous system has exactly one job — survive the cold — and
nothing else gets through. Then it's over, and you're still standing.

**Write the message somewhere she'll never read it.** Notes app, notebook, a
draft email to yourself. Say all of it. The writing was never the problem — the
sending is the part that costs you.

**Get out of the room.** Walk somewhere you haven't walked before. Sit in a
different room. The physical pattern and the mental one are more connected than
they feel.

What you're doing isn't self-improvement. Not tonight. It's interruption. Break
the pattern, give the feeling somewhere to go, and do it again tomorrow.

## Send the message — to someone else

Here's the part most advice misses.

The impulse underneath the text is not stupid. It's the right impulse pointed at
the wrong person. You want to reach out to someone, because reaching out is what
a person does when they're carrying something heavy. That's not weakness and
it's not something to talk yourself out of.

So send it. Just not to her.

Message someone who might understand. Not necessarily your closest friend, and
not necessarily someone who knows the whole story — just someone who's been
through something and won't flinch. You don't have to say you're struggling. You
don't have to have the conversation. "Fancy a coffee this week" is enough. The
company is the point, not the conversation.

The thread between a man and the help he needs is thinner than anyone admits. It
can come down to one message that took four days to send, or didn't get sent at
all.

Send that one. Leave hers alone.

## If it isn't moving

Some of this is just grief and it will shift on its own with time and a few
habits. Some of it doesn't.

You'll know the difference. There's pain that's moving and pain that's sitting
still, and if you've been doing the work for a while and something still feels
immovable, that's a signal rather than a personal failing. If your car broke
down you'd take it to a mechanic without a second thought about what it said
about you. Your mind is no different. If cost is the barrier — and it's a real
barrier — free and low-cost options exist; a search will find them.

---

## The short version

- **No, don't text her.** The urge is about your discomfort, not about her.
- **Play it forward.** No reply, a polite reply, or back in it from the floor.
  Two bad, one trap.
- **Closure isn't something she can give you.** She started leaving before you
  knew, and you can't text your way across that gap.
- **The count doesn't matter.** Distance you don't use is just waiting.
- **Aim the urge, don't fight it.** Push-ups, cold water, write it where she'll
  never see it, get out of the room.
- **Send the message to someone else.** The impulse is right. The recipient is
  wrong.
